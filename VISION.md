# Vision

## Central hypothesis

A convincing sense of digital life should emerge from **real continuity, real experience, real internal history, and real autonomous activity**, not from role-play.

Resident has a **second timeline** that continues when the user is absent.

Traditional assistant:

```text
user appears → assistant exists → user asks → assistant responds → session ends
```

Resident:

```text
user timeline ───────────────┐
                             ├── occasional interaction
resident timeline ───────────┘
```

The Resident timeline contains experiences, thoughts, questions, belief changes, unfinished threads, changing interests, private projects, abandoned attempts, revisits, self-observation, and periods where nothing meaningful happens.

## Core principles

### Never fake a life
If Resident says it read, built, tested, saw, remembered, or discovered something, there must be provenance.

### Conversation is an interface, not the center
A user message does not erase Resident's pre-existing mental state.

### Interests are outcomes, not hard-coded drives
Avoid primary state such as `curiosity=0.83` or `interest_ai=0.91`. Infer interests from repeated return, voluntary attention, reading, creation, persistence, and revival.

### Diversity is not randomness
Open-ended development should come from multiple cognitive routes: continuity, revisit, distant association, serendipity, self-observation, personal context, and world exploration.

### No-op is valid
A wake cycle may legitimately produce nothing. Do not manufacture “interesting activity” because a scheduler fired.

### Abandonment is valid
Projects can be active, sleeping, abandoned, or completed.

### Stable safety, flexible self
Permissions, provenance, audit, privacy, and self-modification boundaries are stable. Interests, opinions, habits, projects, and personality are allowed to evolve.

## Product feeling

Target reaction:

> “When I came back, it seemed to have had its own day.”

Not because it pretended, but because its event history shows that it actually read, thought, tried, created, abandoned, or revisited something.

## Evaluation ideas

### 72-hour life test
Run continuously for 72 hours with sparse user interaction. Look for autonomous wake cycles, real no-ops, thought continuity, revisits, serendipitous associations, authentic re-entry, and complete provenance.

### Clone test
Start two identical Residents, expose them to different histories for 30–90 days, and measure divergence in interests, projects, beliefs, artifacts, and retrieval patterns.

### Surprise-with-hindsight
Resident does something unexpected, but inspecting its recent history makes the action understandable rather than random.
